### Question and why

The research log's questions 1–3, which together are step 1:

1. Do following-evaluator@2 and assessment-evaluator@1 reproduce independently
   hand-worked oracle cases covering every rule of the contract?
2. On stage 1, where do the clock and the frozen `online-time-warp@8`, `@12` and `@14`
   fail under the new measures, and do the failures show the forward-only,
   tempo-clamped design the contract predicts?

that cannot see a known defect is wrong (question 2). The gates are placeholders until

### Baselines and how their positions map to events

`clock-follower@1`, `online-time-warp@8`, `@12` and `@14`, run unchanged through
`seam/legacy.ts` with the registry's abilities (the clock the whole score, the others
the first four measures), handed `{ from: top, parts: all, 90 per minute, rate 1 }` at
48 kHz in 480-sample chunks by `seam/runner.ts`. They emit continuous positions in
quarters, which the adapter converts with Studio's `scorePositionAt`, holding a claim
past the end at the final boundary.

**The scored mapping** is the contract's rule 1: a position shows the **last event whose
onset it has reached**, comparing `(ordinal, metricOffset)`. So a cursor 0.01 quarter
short of an onset is still on the previous event, and the final boundary shows the last
event. Lag is forgiven up to D = 0.2 s by the grace rule; running ahead of an onset is
not.

**A diagnostic mapping**, reported beside and never scored: each position is replaced by
the event whose onset quarter is nearest to it (ties to the later event) before the same
evaluation. It measures how much the choice of mapping, rather than the listener,
decides a baseline's score.

The baselines produce no assessment, so assessment-evaluator@1 is exercised only by its
oracle. Causality is checked once per listener per score, on the example at the handed
tempo, by `seamCausality` (six prefix checks). Cost is `executeSeam`'s.

### Predictions

The clock's numbers follow from the definitions (it ignores the audio), so predictions
2–5 check the runner and evaluator together. The online time warpers' are genuine
predictions.

| # | Prediction |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both evaluators reproduce every number in event-oracle@1 without an erratum. |
| 2 | Clock at 90: on event ≥ 99% of supported answerable time on both scores, ahead 0, by event 100%. |
| 3 | Clock at 99: s1 on event 100%, by event 4/4; s2 behind 10–14% (the clock reaches events 4–7 more than 0.2 s late), by event 4/8. |
| 4 | Clock at 63: ahead 42–52% on s1 and 70–78% on s2. |
| 5 | Clock at 45: ahead 60–70% on s1 and 78–88% on s2. |
| 6 | Each time warper at 90 and 99: on event ≥ 85%, ahead ≤ 5% on both scores. By event may miss event 0 during the 0.2 s warm-up. |
| 7 | The contract's prediction: at 45, every time warper fails visibly on at least one score, with on event < 80% from ahead time ≥ 10% or abstention ≥ 20%. |
| 8 | At 45 and 63, every time warper has less ahead time than the clock on every example. |
| 9 | Under the diagnostic nearest mapping, the clock at 90 is ahead ≥ 40% of the time on both scores: the mapping, not the listener, would decide its score. |
| 10 | Every causality check passes; each listener's sustained cost ratio is ≤ 0.25. |

Contradictions: any oracle number the evaluators cannot reproduce (1); a clock figure
outside its derived range, which would point at the runner, adapter or evaluator rather
than the clock (2–5); a time warper below 85% on event or above 5% ahead at 90 or 99 (6);
every time warper at 45 with on event ≥ 90% and ahead ≤ 5% on both scores (contradicts 7
and the contract's expectation); the nearest mapping changing the clock's score at 90 by
less than 10 points (9).

### Decision rules, fixed now

- **D1, the instruments.** They are trusted if the bench test reproduces every oracle
  number. A disagreement is diagnosed. An evaluator defect is fixed in the evaluator.
  If the hand-work is wrong by the contract's own rules, the correction is a new oracle
  version (event-oracle@2) with the arithmetic and the rule shown, recorded in this
  report; an oracle number is never changed to match an evaluator's output. If a rule
  itself turns out ambiguous, the instruments contract gets a new version, and the
  change is recorded. If the evaluators still disagree after that, stop: question 1 is
  unresolved and nothing is rendered or run (the split above).
- **D2, stage 1 and the baselines** are recorded whatever they show; no baseline is
  tuned, rerun with other settings or dropped. A refusal at `start` is a valid answer,
  recorded as a refusal. A crash or a failed prefix check is an infrastructure or
  causality finding: the failed attempt is preserved and diagnosed before any rerun,
  which is a new run ID.
- **D3, question 2.** The new measures *see the known defect* if predictions 4 and 5
  hold (the clock's ahead time at slow tempi). The contract's prediction about the
  forward-only, tempo-clamped path is *supported* if 7 holds, *contradicted* if every
  time warper at 45 has on event ≥ 90% and ahead ≤ 5% on both scores, and *mixed*
  otherwise, with the pattern recorded. Differences between v8 and v12/v14 are
  reported; stage 1 has no deviation, so no conclusion about smoothing is drawn from it.
- **D4, gates: proposed, never adopted.** After the run, one number is proposed for each
  placeholder in the contract (and for θ), chosen so that (a) every oracle record meant
  to be correct passes, (b) every oracle record built to show a defect fails on the
  measure that shows it, (c) the clock fails stage 1 at 45 and 63, and (d) no value is
  moved to make a particular time warper pass or fail. The proposal goes in this report
  and is flagged in the research log as **awaiting the user's approval**. Nothing in
  the contract changes.
- **Inconclusive.** Observations that fit no branch above are recorded as inconclusive,
  with no favourable branch invented afterwards.
