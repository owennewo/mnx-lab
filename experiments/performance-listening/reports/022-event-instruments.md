### Question and why

The research log's questions 1–3, which together are step 1:

1. Do following-evaluator@2 and assessment-evaluator@1 reproduce independently
   hand-worked oracle cases covering every rule of the contract?
2. On stage 1, where do the clock and the frozen `online-time-warp@8`, `@12` and `@14`
   fail under the new measures, and do the failures show the forward-only,
   tempo-clamped design the contract predicts?
3. Which numerical gates should the user approve?

Nothing can be judged until the instruments are trusted (question 1), and an instrument
that cannot see a known defect is wrong (question 2). The gates are placeholders until
both are answered (question 3).

**One experiment, not split.** Stage 1 is eight clips of under a minute of audio in all,
rendered with the existing sine renderer, and the baselines run unchanged through the
existing legacy adapter, so rendering and measuring them is small beside the
instruments. The pre-registered fallback: if the evaluators do not reproduce the frozen
oracle (rule D1 below), nothing is rendered or run, question 1 is recorded as

### Baselines and how their positions map to events

`clock-follower@1`, `online-time-warp@8`, `@12` and `@14`, run unchanged through
`seam/legacy.ts` with the registry's abilities (the clock the whole score, the others
the first four measures), handed `{ from: top, parts: all, 90 per minute, rate 1 }` at
48 kHz in 480-sample chunks by `seam/runner.ts`. They emit continuous positions in
quarters, which the adapter converts with Studio's `scorePositionAt`, holding a claim
past the end at the final boundary.

**The scored mapping** is the contract's rule 1: a position shows the **last event whose
onset it has reached**, comparing `(ordinal, metricOffset)`. So a cursor 0.01 quarter
short of an onset is still on the previous event, and the final boundary shows the last
event. Lag is forgiven up to D = 0.2 s by the grace rule; running ahead of an onset is
not.

**A diagnostic mapping**, reported beside and never scored: each position is replaced by
the event whose onset quarter is nearest to it (ties to the later event) before the same
evaluation. It measures how much the choice of mapping, rather than the listener,
decides a baseline's score.

The baselines produce no assessment, so assessment-evaluator@1 is exercised only by its
oracle. Causality is checked once per listener per score, on the example at the handed
tempo, by `seamCausality` (six prefix checks). Cost is `executeSeam`'s.

### Decision rules, fixed now

- **D1, the instruments.** They are trusted if the bench test reproduces every oracle
  number. A disagreement is diagnosed. An evaluator defect is fixed in the evaluator.
  If the hand-work is wrong by the contract's own rules, the correction is a new oracle
  version (event-oracle@2) with the arithmetic and the rule shown, recorded in this
  report; an oracle number is never changed to match an evaluator's output. If a rule
  itself turns out ambiguous, the instruments contract gets a new version, and the
  change is recorded. If the evaluators still disagree after that, stop: question 1 is
  unresolved and nothing is rendered or run (the split above).
- **D2, stage 1 and the baselines** are recorded whatever they show; no baseline is
  tuned, rerun with other settings or dropped. A refusal at `start` is a valid answer,
  recorded as a refusal. A crash or a failed prefix check is an infrastructure or
  causality finding: the failed attempt is preserved and diagnosed before any rerun,
  which is a new run ID.
- **D3, question 2.** The new measures *see the known defect* if predictions 4 and 5
  hold (the clock's ahead time at slow tempi). The contract's prediction about the
  forward-only, tempo-clamped path is *supported* if 7 holds, *contradicted* if every
  time warper at 45 has on event ≥ 90% and ahead ≤ 5% on both scores, and *mixed*
  otherwise, with the pattern recorded. Differences between v8 and v12/v14 are
  reported; stage 1 has no deviation, so no conclusion about smoothing is drawn from it.
- **D4, gates: proposed, never adopted.** After the run, one number is proposed for each
  placeholder in the contract (and for θ), chosen so that (a) every oracle record meant
  to be correct passes, (b) every oracle record built to show a defect fails on the
  measure that shows it, (c) the clock fails stage 1 at 45 and 63, and (d) no value is
  moved to make a particular time warper pass or fail. The proposal goes in this report
  and is flagged in the research log as **awaiting the user's approval**. Nothing in
  the contract changes.
- **Inconclusive.** Observations that fit no branch above are recorded as inconclusive,
  with no favourable branch invented afterwards.

- **D2:** the baselines are recorded as they ran; none was tuned, rerun or dropped.
- **D3:** the new measures **see the known defect**: the clock's ahead time at 63 and 45

### Proposed gates, awaiting the user's approval

Each value was checked against rules (a)–(d) of D4. Every oracle record meant to be
correct passes. Every record built to show a defect fails, on the measure that shows it.
The clock fails stage 1 at 45 and 63. No value was moved for a time warper.

**The live cursor, on every example of a stage:**

| Measure | Proposed | Placeholder | What in the oracle needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| On event, of supported answerable time | ≥ 95% | 95% | F6-B (an extra note moves it early) reaches 94.9% |
| Ahead | ≤ 1% | 1% | F9 with a too-narrow sync band gives 1.9%, F6-B 5.1%; a correct cursor gives 0 |
| Exposure, of answerable time | ≤ 5% | 5% | F6-B 5.1% |
| Longest exposure episode | ≤ 0.5 s | 0.5 s | F2-C 1.0 s, F3-C 0.9 s |
| By event, reached within 0.2 s | ≥ 95%, so every event on an example with fewer than 20 | 95% | F3-D and F4-C (late after a distinguishing event) and F7-B (late live, right in hindsight) pass every time measure and fail only here |
| Causality, sustained cost ratio, chunk p99 | every check; ≤ 0.25; ≤ 10 ms | contract 1's | |

**Pooled over each stage** rather than per example, because a one-deviation example
holds one such case: **recovery after a missing event ≥ 90%** (placeholder 90%) and,
proposed new, **extra notes held ≥ 90%**.

**The assessment:** per example, **overall tempo within ±5%**, **every expected interval
reported and within ±10%** (placeholders ±5% and ±10%; "every interval reported" is new,
because A5-B otherwise passes having skipped two), and **every score note assessed**
(new, from A1-C). Pooled over each stage, for each kind of finding (slow and fast flags,
missing, wrong and dead notes): **≥ 90% found and false alarms ≤ 5% of negatives**
(placeholders). On an eight-note example, one false alarm is 12.5%, so the happy path
must be clean. **θ = 0.10**, with the optional band from θ/2, is proposed as defined.

Under this proposal the oracle's correct records all pass and each defect record fails.
On stage 1, no baseline passes, even on the cursor gates alone (none makes an
assessment). The clock meets the cursor gates on three examples: both at 90 and s1 at
99. v8 meets them on the same three, and v12 and v14 only on the two at 90.

### For the user: three decisions the evidence raises

1. **The gates above.**
2. **What "the player's own tempo" is for flags.** Oracle case A3 applies the
   contract's definition to a 3 s hesitation in two bars. The overall tempo falls to 42,
   so the steady first bar reads **fast** (ratio 1.43) as well as the second **slow**. A
   report that says only "slowed in bar 2" misses a required flag. If the user meant
   variation relative to the player's typical tempo, the reference could be the median
   local tempo, which is 60 here. That would be a new version of the instruments and a
   change to the contract, so it is the user's call.
3. **Dead notes in the Studio vocabulary.** Vocabulary 2 has no `dead` verdict, so
   assessment-report@1 reads a `substitution` with no heard pitch as dead. A distinct
   verdict would be a vocabulary change for SEAM part 2.

## Next

Advice, not a rule. Experiment 023 is the contract's step 2: research event-based
following first (score followers with explicit stay and skip transitions), then build
the simplest live cursor and end-of-piece assessor that pass stage 1, judged under the
gates the user approves. Three things this run suggests:

- A listener that moves only on evidence of an onset never runs ahead. Every baseline
  failure on stage 1 was ahead time, so that is the cheapest thing to get right.
- The 0.2 s grace forgives lag and the first event's deadline is tight: the time
  warpers' ten-frame warm-up lands exactly on it.
- Stage 1 at the handed tempo cannot tell a listener from a clock; the off-tempo
  examples carry stage 1.
