Question and why

The research log's questions 1–3, which together are step 1:

1. Do following-evaluator@2 and assessment-evaluator@1 reproduce independently hand-worked oracle cases covering every rule of the contract? 2. On stage 1, where do the clock and the frozen online-time-warp@8, @12 and @14 fail under the new measures, and do the failures show the forward-only, tempo-clamped design the contract predicts? 3. Which numerical gates should the user approve?

Nothing can be judged until the instruments are trusted (question 1), and an instrument that cannot see a known defect is wrong (question 2). The gates are placeholders until both are answered (question 3).

One experiment, not split. Stage 1 is eight clips of under a minute of audio in all, rendered with the existing sine renderer, and the baselines run unchanged through the existing legacy adapter, so rendering and measuring them is small beside the instruments. The pre-registered fallback: if the evaluators do not reproduce the frozen oracle (rule D1 below), nothing is rendered or run, question 1 is recorded as unresolved, and rendering and baselines become experiment 023's first part.

Stage 1

  • Scores: the committed one bar, C4–F4 and two-bar scale.
  • Sound: one sine synth, mixSines from ladder/render.ts unchanged: one sine per note, 10 ms linear attack and release, −12 dBFS peak, each note sounding its written length so consecutive notes abut. The clip ends when the last note does.
  • Performances: perfect, steady, handed 90 quarters per minute; played at 45, 63, 90 and 99 (50%, 70%, 100%, 110%): the beginner, intermediate and advanced ranges of the contract's categories, half speed included. 2 scores × 4 tempi = 8 examples. No deviations and no negative controls: stage 1 is perfect performances, and deviations are stage 2.
  • Labels: generated by the perfect-performance labeller from Studio's compiled performance: events, note keys, sample-exact onsets and ends, every note matched, event-resolution segments with singleton admissible sets. It refuses a score with two consecutive events of identical pitches, because it implements no ambiguity rule.
  • Freezing: a new private set, contract2-stage1-v1, beside the old ones in /home/williao/dev/mnx-listening-data/: WAVs, labels and a manifest whose hash is recorded in freeze.json; it is never overwritten.

Baselines and how their positions map to events

clock-follower@1, online-time-warp@8, @12 and @14, run unchanged through seam/legacy.ts with the registry's abilities (the clock the whole score, the others the first four measures), handed { from: top, parts: all, 90 per minute, rate 1 } at 48 kHz in 480-sample chunks by seam/runner.ts. They emit continuous positions in quarters, which the adapter converts with Studio's scorePositionAt, holding a claim past the end at the final boundary.

The scored mapping is the contract's rule 1: a position shows the last event whose onset it has reached, comparing (ordinal, metricOffset). So a cursor 0.01 quarter short of an onset is still on the previous event, and the final boundary shows the last event. Lag is forgiven up to D = 0.2 s by the grace rule; running ahead of an onset is not.

A diagnostic mapping, reported beside and never scored: each position is replaced by the event whose onset quarter is nearest to it (ties to the later event) before the same evaluation. It measures how much the choice of mapping, rather than the listener, decides a baseline's score.

The baselines produce no assessment, so assessment-evaluator@1 is exercised only by its oracle. Causality is checked once per listener per score, on the example at the handed tempo, by seamCausality (six prefix checks). Cost is executeSeam's.

Decision rules, fixed now

  • D1, the instruments. They are trusted if the bench test reproduces every oracle number. A disagreement is diagnosed. An evaluator defect is fixed in the evaluator. If the hand-work is wrong by the contract's own rules, the correction is a new oracle version (event-oracle@2) with the arithmetic and the rule shown, recorded in this report; an oracle number is never changed to match an evaluator's output. If a rule itself turns out ambiguous, the instruments contract gets a new version, and the change is recorded. If the evaluators still disagree after that, stop: question 1 is unresolved and nothing is rendered or run (the split above).
  • D2, stage 1 and the baselines are recorded whatever they show; no baseline is tuned, rerun with other settings or dropped. A refusal at start is a valid answer, recorded as a refusal. A crash or a failed prefix check is an infrastructure or causality finding: the failed attempt is preserved and diagnosed before any rerun, which is a new run ID.
  • D3, question 2. The new measures *see the known defect* if predictions 4 and 5 hold (the clock's ahead time at slow tempi). The contract's prediction about the forward-only, tempo-clamped path is *supported* if 7 holds, *contradicted* if every time warper at 45 has on event ≥ 90% and ahead ≤ 5% on both scores, and *mixed* otherwise, with the pattern recorded. Differences between v8 and v12/v14 are reported; stage 1 has no deviation, so no conclusion about smoothing is drawn from it.
  • D4, gates: proposed, never adopted. After the run, one number is proposed for each placeholder in the contract (and for θ), chosen so that (a) every oracle record meant to be correct passes, (b) every oracle record built to show a defect fails on the measure that shows it, (c) the clock fails stage 1 at 45 and 63, and (d) no value is moved to make a particular time warper pass or fail. The proposal goes in this report and is flagged in the research log as awaiting the user's approval. Nothing in the contract changes.
  • Inconclusive. Observations that fit no branch above are recorded as inconclusive, with no favourable branch invented afterwards.

For the user: three decisions the evidence raises

1. The gates above. 2. What "the player's own tempo" is for flags. Oracle case A3 applies the contract's definition to a 3 s hesitation in two bars. The overall tempo falls to 42, so the steady first bar reads fast (ratio 1.43) as well as the second slow. A report that says only "slowed in bar 2" misses a required flag. If the user meant variation relative to the player's typical tempo, the reference could be the median local tempo, which is 60 here. That would be a new version of the instruments and a change to the contract, so it is the user's call. 3. Dead notes in the Studio vocabulary. Vocabulary 2 has no dead verdict, so assessment-report@1 reads a substitution with no heard pitch as dead. A distinct verdict would be a vocabulary change for SEAM part 2.

Next

Advice, not a rule. Experiment 023 is the contract's step 2: research event-based following first (score followers with explicit stay and skip transitions), then build the simplest live cursor and end-of-piece assessor that pass stage 1, judged under the gates the user approves. Three things this run suggests:

  • A listener that moves only on evidence of an onset never runs ahead. Every baseline failure on stage 1 was ahead time, so that is the cheapest thing to get right.
  • The 0.2 s grace forgives lag and the first event's deadline is tight: the time warpers' ten-frame warm-up lands exactly on it.
  • Stage 1 at the handed tempo cannot tell a listener from a clock; the off-tempo examples carry stage 1.
