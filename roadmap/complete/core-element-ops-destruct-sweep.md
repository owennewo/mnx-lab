>

> selection rung — so its agreement block is not the contract's op pair / key / rung

> evidence then rides on.

> declaring the primitive classes it claims) and the reference map
> (`src/model/references.ts` — 15 join kinds across 8 id spaces); the ink-census
> join (`harness/conformance/element-census.test.ts`) proving every one of the 63
> drawn classes is claimed or declared structural; the corpus sweep
> (`harness/conformance/destruct-sweep.test.ts`) with the two verdict axes and the
> six oracles; the committed report (`harness/reports/destruct-sweep.json`, written
> by `npm run sweep:destruct`) and its drift test; and the fixes the sweep caught —
> `deleteNote` now unlinks both ends (ties, slur endpoint pins, technique
> relationships) and cascades the beams, and `driveToElement` resolves the slot
> instead of comparing coordinates.
>
> **First baseline: 1,460 elements over 106 scenarios — 639 removed, 821 no-op, 162
> unaddressable notes**, and every scenario passes its invariants. Goldens
> byte-identical; the suite went 4 s → 9.7 s (110 sweep tests inside it).
>
> **Where it stands 2026-08-15: 1,441 removed, 19 refused, 0 no-op, 0
> unaddressable.** The 19 refusals are the correct answer (containers still
> holding ink, a meter-changing time signature, a referenced kit component);
> the report's `findings` array is empty for the first time.

## The four decisions

1. **What counts as an element** — a document walk per kind, with the renderer's own
   ink census as its completeness oracle, and the choice/consequence rule drawing the
   line between an element and structure.
2. **The verdict vocabulary** — two independent axes (addressed? removed?) plus the
   oracle verdict, and exactly one of them may redden a build.
3. **The oracles** — item 1's five, widened: validity becomes *relative*, references
   grow past ties, and a new **surviving-document** oracle enforces the campaign's
   "everything else byte-identical except forced cascades".
4. **The artifact** — a committed report of rollups + findings, regenerated by a
   script, with a drift test in `npm test`; the per-element detail is a run-time
   affordance, never a committed dump.

## What item 1 left, measured

The v0 walker sees keyed notes of `parts[0]`, staff 1. Against the corpus that is a
minority report — surveyed 2026-08-14 over all 106 scenarios:

| The corpus holds | Count | Where |
|---|---|---|
| notes | **801** | 102 scenarios (4 hold none: two layout-only, the tab canvas, the percussion kit) |
| notes in `parts[1..]` | 100 | 7 scenarios (twelve-bar-blues 48, multiple-layouts 22) |
| notes in a second voice | 93 | 9 scenarios |
| notes on staff > 1 | 22 | 3 scenarios (grand-staff 16) |
| container content (tuplet / grace / tremolo / `space`) | 15 items | 9 scenarios |
| rests | 87 | *not elements* (§8.11: a rest is absence) |
| tie → note references | 12 | 3 scenarios |
| beam → event references | 98 | 10 scenarios |
| slurs / technique | 3 / 5 scenarios | the reference classes the oracle has never met |

So roughly **215 notes sit outside the v0 walker's reach** (the three overlapping
groups above), and the dangling-reference oracle — written in item 1, never once
fired — has 110+ real references waiting for it.

## Decision 1 — what counts as an element

The campaign's definition is "anything the renderer draws distinguishable ink for".
That is a claim about the *renderer*, so let the renderer answer it. A census of all
101 committed `expected.primitives.json` goldens: **5,406 primitives carrying 63
distinct class tokens**.

```
staff-line 1850   notehead 874*  stem 743    barline 538   ledger-line 241
clef 166   barline-final-thin/thick 300   time-sig-num/den 270   rest 89
beam 55    fret-bg 55*  fret-number 55*  dynamic 41   barline-start 40
flag 38    grace 30   bracket 30   lyric 28   staff-label 26   key-sig 25
repeat-dot 20  tab-clef 20  ending 18  dot 15  brace 15  repeat-end 14
diagnostic-marker/validation 28  tie 13  direction 13  accidental 11
section-label 11  score-title 10  group-label 9  articulation 8
tuplet-bracket 8  ending-label 7  tremolo-beam 7  rehearsal-box/label 14
multirest-cap 6  repeat-start 6  slur 6  source-label 6  tab-tuning-letter 6
grace-slash 5  segno 5  lyric-hyphen 5  jump 4  tempo 4  fine 3
multirest-bar/count 6  tremolo 3  tuplet-number 3  rest-full-measure 2
ottava 2  time-sig 2  clef-change 1  ottava-label 1  repeat-times 1  tab-capo 1
                                          (* = the only classes carrying a sourceId)
```

Two things fall straight out.

**The provenance gap is exact.** Of 5,406 drawn primitives, **821 carry a `sourceId`
— and every one of them is a note** (`notehead`, `fret-number`, `fret-bg`). Ink for a
clef, a dynamic, a rehearsal mark or a slur is drawn anonymously: nothing in the
render output points back at the document node that caused it. That is why the walker
**cannot** simply be derived from primitives, however attractive "the golden IS the
element inventory" sounds — deriving it needs `sourceId` on every drawn thing, a
renderer-wide provenance project this item refuses to become.

**So: the walk is document-side, the census is its oracle.** The walker enumerates
per kind from the document (the shape item 1 already has, generalized), and a static
**ink-census join** asserts that every non-structural class drawn anywhere in the
corpus maps to a walker kind — or sits on an explicit structural allowlist with a
reason. The join is what turns "element = distinguishable ink" from a slogan into a
machine-checked claim, in the same discipline as the keymap-docs joins and the
`coversDefs` coverage map. Its by-product is a ranked list of the classes nobody can
name yet, which is exactly the campaign's ordering evidence.

**Element or structure? The choice/consequence rule.** The repo already owns the
principle that sorts this, from [spec-instrument-position.md](../proposed/spec-instrument-position.md):
*encode the choice, not the consequence*. Ink whose existence is a **consequence** of
other data is structure and is never independently removable — staff lines, ledger
lines, stems, flags, `barline-final-*`, `fret-bg`, `lyric-hyphen`, `multirest-*`,
`ending-label`, `rehearsal-box`. Ink whose existence records a **choice** in the
document is an element — a clef, a dynamic, a lyric syllable, a section label, a
tuplet. The rule also resolves the awkward cases cleanly: a **beam** is drawn ink
*and* authored data, so the element is the document's `beams[]` entry (addressed
through its ink), not the drawn stroke; a **`fret-number`** is a consequence of the
note's string choice, so removing it means removing the annotation, not the ink.
Where the rule is genuinely ambiguous, the census row is the agenda item for the
family that owns it (items 4–13), not a decision this item makes alone.

## Decision 2 — the verdict vocabulary

Per element, **two independent axes** — conflating them was the temptation, and it
would have hidden exactly the distinction the campaign is built on:

- **address**: `addressed` (cursor navigation reached it) | `unaddressable` (no
  selection can name it — a *ladder* finding, owned by the ladder's per-level pass and
  campaign item 13, never fixed here)
- **removal**: `removed` (op applied, all oracles pass) | `no-op` (nothing in the op
  union can remove this kind — **the campaign's work queue**) | `refused` (an op
  exists and correctly declined, e.g. a container still holding ink) | `broken` (a
  removal happened and an oracle failed)

**Only `broken` reddens the build.** Everything else is a recorded verdict, because
today most of the corpus is honestly not destructible — one ink op against ~50
non-structural ink classes — and a harness that fails for saying so would be deleted
within a week. `no-op` and `unaddressable` are the scoreboard; `broken` is a bug.

A sixth possibility is deliberately absent: destruct has **no expected-unreachable
class**. The construct harness needs one (the five `expect.standard: invalid`
spec-gap exhibits must never become authorable), but an invalid document's ink is
still ink and still deletable — the asymmetry is real and worth stating once. What
those five scenarios *do* change is the validity oracle, below.

## Decision 3 — the oracles, widened

Item 1's set transfers unchanged in shape; three grow:

1. **The deletion applies** and the document changes. (unchanged)
2. **Validity is relative, not absolute.** "Still schema-valid" is wrong for the five
   invalid-by-design scenarios, which start invalid on purpose and whose pinned error
   fragments are the point. The oracle becomes *the error set does not grow* —
   deleting ink may fix an error, never add one. Same rule, strictly stronger,
   applies everywhere.
3. **No new diagnostics** beyond the untouched document's baseline. (unchanged —
   already relative, which is why it generalizes for free)
4. **No dangling references**, widened from `ties[].target` to every id-bearing
   reference the corpus actually holds: ties (12), slurs (3 scenarios), technique
   targets (5), and **beam → event references (98)**. The last is the interesting
   one: `deleteNote` turns an emptied event into a rest of the same duration, so the
   event id still *resolves* — the beam does not dangle, it beams a rest. The oracle
   therefore checks resolution **and** that every reference target still draws the
   ink the reference presumes; the fix is a forced cascade (drop the event from
   `beams[].events`, drop the beam below two events), declared as such.
5. **Undo restores the loaded document byte-identically.** (unchanged — the strongest
   oracle we have, and free)
6. **NEW — the surviving document.** The campaign's goal sentence promises
   "everything else surviving byte-identically except forced cascades", and nothing
   has ever asserted it. The oracle: diff the document before and after, and require
   every change to lie inside the element's own subtree or a **declared** cascade for
   that removal class. This is the no-tombstone rule made mechanical — a
   `staffPosition: 0` residue, an orphaned minted tie id, or a helpfully-rewritten
   sibling all fail it, and every later item inherits the check for free.

**A free metadata audit falls out of oracle 4.** `meta.idRefs` claims to record
whether a scenario exercises id-based cross-referencing; only 5 scenarios set it,
and none of them are `spec/ties`, `spec/tie-targets` or the slur mirrors, which
plainly do. The sweep's scan is ground truth, so the mismatch is reported as a
finding — not a failure, since `scenarios/spec/` is `sync:spec`-owned and the fix
there belongs to the generator.

## Decision 4 — the artifact

The campaign's rule for this axis is that the walk is generative and **the report is
the durable artifact** — no fixtures, nothing to record by hand. Shape:

- **`npm run sweep:destruct`** runs the walk over the corpus and writes
  **`harness/reports/destruct-sweep.json`**, committed. It holds **rollups**
  (scenario × kind → verdict counts) and **findings** (every `broken`,
  `unaddressable`, and the metadata mismatches, each named by scenario and address).
  It deliberately does *not* dump the ~800 successes: a per-element commit would be
  half a megabyte of churn, and a diff nobody reads asserts nothing.
- **`npm test` recomputes the sweep and compares against the committed report** —
  the `update:primitives` discipline exactly. Drift in **either** direction is red:
  a new failure, or an improvement nobody recorded. So an item that lands a removal
  verb sees its rows flip from `no-op` to `removed` in a reviewable diff, and that
  diff is the item's evidence.
- The workbench view (a destructibility column on `#/objects`, or a sweep page) is
  **deferred** — the panel button already runs the walk per scenario, which covers
  the hands-on need, and the coverage-map question belongs with item 3.

**Cost, measured** (2026-08-14, this machine): a full per-element cycle — fresh
session, address by navigation, delete, both-projection layout, schema validate,
undo-all — runs **10–14 ms**, near-flat across document sizes (`spec/ties` 12.4 ms,
`spec/beams` 10.0, `navigation-playground` 10.4, `twelve-bar-blues` 13.9). So the
801 notes cost **≈9–11 s**, and the suite goes from 4 s to roughly 15 s. Enumerating
non-note kinds is nearly free until ops exist for them: a `no-op` element never
triggers a layout or a validation. That is affordable now; if it bites later, shard
the sweep by scenario across vitest files rather than sampling the corpus — the tail
is where the gaps are (the same argument that rejected exemplar-only coverage).

## Scope boundary

This item **generalizes enumeration, addressing and verdicts. It adds no removal
op and grows no rung.** A clef enumerated as `no-op` is a correct outcome here, and
becomes `removed` when item 5 lands. Two exceptions, both because the sweep is what
catches them:

- **The `deleteNote` reference bug** — the known latent one (delete the second note
  of `spec/ties` and the first note's tie dangles). Its fix is the campaign's
  *reference* removal class: unlink **both** ends.
- **The beam cascade** above, for the same reason.

Goldens stay byte-identical throughout: the sweep loads, mutates and discards
in-memory sessions, and never writes `scenarios/`.

## What the build taught (2026-08-14)

- **The census join caught its own author on its first run.** `grace` was both
  claimed by the grace kind and declared structural. It is a *size modifier token*
  (`notehead grace`, `stem grace`) — the container's only ink of its own is the
  slash. The "claimed AND structural" assertion exists precisely because that
  mistake is easy, and it found one immediately.
- **The addressability oracle was wrong, and hid a wrong-note deletion.** Item 1's
  `driveToElement` declared success by comparing cursor coordinates. In
  twelve-bar-blues m10 that "arrived" at the melody note and then deleted **the
  bass note of the other voice**: `EditorCursor` is `{measure, onset, line}` with no
  voice component, so `slotAt` returns whichever slot matches the line first.
  Resolving the slot and comparing its key is the honest test, and it turns a
  silent mis-edit into a reported finding. **The ladder owns the fix** — this is
  concrete evidence for a voice component in the cursor, filed to
  [core-selection-ladder.md](../complete/core-selection-ladder.md).
- **Addressability is projection-dependent.** Two chord members can derive onto the
  same *string* (navigation-playground's first bar puts both on string 1 — itself a
  tab derivation worth a look, since one string cannot sound two notes) while their
  staff positions separate them cleanly in notation. Driving both projections
  before giving up cut 6 false findings, so the report describes the editor's real
  reach rather than one pane's.
- **The reference bug was four bugs, not one.** The campaign predicted a dangling
  `ties[].target`. Disabling the fix and re-running names the full set — **13
  scenarios across 4 classes**: ties (`spec/ties`, `spec/tie-targets`), slurs
  (`spec/slurs`, `spec/slurs-targeting-specific-notes`), technique relationships
  (`lab/tab-techniques/{slides,hammer-pull-chain}` — a hammer-on to a deleted note),
  and 7 beam scenarios going **inkless** rather than dangling, exactly as predicted:
  the emptied event keeps its id, so the beam does not break, it beams a rest.
- **`no-op` dominates, and that is the point**: 821 of 1,460 elements. The corpus
  exercises 44 of the 45 declared kinds, and **one** of them has a removal verb. The
  per-kind rollup is now the campaign's ordering evidence — clef 113,
  time-signature 99, part-name 59, dynamic 43, beam 40, string-annotation 34,
  lyric 28. (The unexercised kind is `harmony`: the corpus's only `harmonies` block
  sits at the bare `global.measures[0].harmonies` of an invalid-by-design exhibit,
  which is the whole point of that scenario — real documents carry it under
  `_x.mnxLab`, where the walker looks.)
- **Cost came in as predicted**: the sweep adds ~5.5 s (suite 4 s → 9.7 s) for 106
  scenarios, close to the 10–14 ms/element benchmark, because a `no-op` element
  never triggers a layout or a validation.

## Stages

1. **The walker generalized** — all parts, all staves, all voices, container content;
   `elementKeys` grows a kind beyond notes and the two invariants item 1 fixed
   (navigation-only addressing, keys recomputed after every deletion) are preserved.
2. **The ink-census join** — the structural allowlist with reasons, and the assertion
   that every other drawn class maps to a walker kind.
3. **The verdicts and the report** — the two axes, `npm run sweep:destruct`, the
   committed rollups + findings, the drift test.
4. **The widened oracles** — relative validity, the reference scan, the
   surviving-document check; then fix what they catch (`deleteNote` unlinking, the
   beam cascade) and commit the first real baseline.
5. **Learnings → the campaign log** — the report's per-kind counts are items 4–13's
   ordering evidence; item 3 inherits the tiering machinery.

## Open questions

- **Do the ladder gaps get fixed here?** Proposed: no. `unaddressable` is recorded and
  handed to [core-selection-ladder.md](../complete/core-selection-ladder.md)'s
  per-level pass; the sweep's job is to name them, and 215 notes are already named.
- **Does an element need one canonical address, or is any that reaches it enough?** The
  report needs a stable per-element key to be diffable across runs; note keys already
  provide it, non-note kinds need one (proposed: kind + containment path, positional —
  the `noteKeys.ts` pattern generalized).
- **Percussion and layout documents**: `spec/orchestral-layout` has zero measures and
  `lab/percussion/minimal-kit` zero notes, but both draw ink (braces, labels, kit
  glyphs). They are the census join's first honest customers — and the campaign's
  "beyond the campaign" list already parks the families that own them.
