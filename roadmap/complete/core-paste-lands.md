## The invariant

> **A decodable clip always lands.** The only refusals left are an empty
> clipboard and a clip that fails to decode — cases with nothing to land.

Today's paste is a negotiation: the destination's shape holds a veto, and a
mismatch produces a typed refusal. That was the right way to *build* the
pipeline — a refusal is cheaper to revise than a wrong document — but as an
editing surface it makes the user pre-compute the planner's compatibility
table in their head. The reviewed reality: they can't, because half the
inputs (which rung the clip was copied at, what a note clip carries) are
invisible.

The replacement stance: paste is a **write with a well-defined footprint**.
The clip defines *what and how much*; the cursor defines *where*; the
document yields. The license for that authority is the one Cut already used —
one atomic history entry with selection-aware undo, so the worst case is
Ctrl+Z and try something else. Where the result is questionable, it **lands
and is flagged** by the forgiving renderer's existing diagnostics rather
than refused — the repo's own "degrade visibly, never clamp silently" rule,
applied to editing.

## The four rules

Everything below is generated by these; a case not listed resolves by them.

1. **The selection contributes only an anchor.** The cursor already carries
   part / staff / voice / bar / onset / line; every clip kind reads the
   sub-tuple it needs. The destination's *rung* gates nothing and its
   *extent* is ignored — the clip's extent defines the footprint (no tiling;
   Excel taught everyone this model). After the paste, the selection is the
   pasted material at the **clip's** natural rung, which stage 4 already
   does.
2. **Paste overwrites exactly its footprint.** One notehead for a note clip;
   a metric interval in the anchor voice for event/container runs; bar spans
   for the bar rungs; a part slot for a part clip; **everything** for a
   score clip.
3. **Partial coverage consumes the whole unit; leftover time becomes
   rests.** An event half-covered by the footprint's edge is consumed
   entirely; so is a tuplet/grace/tremolo (containers are atomic — they
   cannot be split). The remainder fills with rests using **the same
   spelling Cut's clear-to-rests uses**, so the two authoritative verbs
   cannot drift. Footprints are half-open `[start, end)`: zero-time items at
   the start are consumed, at the end are kept.
4. **The document extends rather than clips.** A footprint overrunning the
   timeline appends bars (inheriting meter; other parts get empty copies).
   A measures/section clip carrying more parts than the destination has
   **creates the missing parts** from the `ClipPartDescriptor[]` the format
   already stores for exactly this bootstrapping — the only thing worse
   than refusing is silently dropping a part's material.

## The case matrix

| Clip | Anchor reads | Lands as |
|---|---|---|
| `note-set` (single, post-floor-axis) | the notehead / nearest line | onto a note: repitch. Onto a chord: replaces the nearest-line member (merge-into-chord stays a future verb). Onto a rest: inks it — the note takes the rest's duration, the one case where the destination donates rhythm, since the clip has none |
| `event-run` | staff/voice + bar + onset | **flows** (D8): the run linearizes against the clip's recorded source meters and re-bins against the destination's — four quarters pasted at beat 3 fill beats 3–4 and continue into the next bar's beats 1–2, in the anchor voice only; gaps stay exactly the silent time they were and touch nothing; boundary leftovers → rests (rule 3); flow off the end → appended bars (rule 4) |
| `container-run` | same | same flow; a zero-time grace clip has a zero-width footprint and inserts at the anchor without consuming time |
| `voice-bars` | staff/voice + bar | overwrites that voice's bar copies for the span, **creating the sequence where the voice does not exist** (decision D4) |
| `staff-bars` | staff + bar | overwrites the anchor staff's bars and staff-owned declarations for the span |
| `part` | — | adds a part below the active one; shorter than the timeline → padded with empty bars, longer → timeline extends (rule 4). The truly-empty-placeholder replacement rule survives |
| `measures` | bar | **overwrites** complete columns from the anchor bar for the span; extends the timeline on overrun; parts map positionally — missing destination parts are created, uncovered destination parts get empty bars in the overwritten columns |
| `section` | bar | as `measures`, with the boundary labels riding their global measures (labels are column data — no special case) |
| `score` | — | **replaces the document.** The footprint of a score is everything; undo restores everything. Replaces today's only-into-empty rule |

## Land-and-flag replaces refuse

Metric and instrument conflicts stop being paste's to adjudicate:

- **Barline-crossing DURATIONS** land byte-true at their onsets. A bar that
  no longer closes gets the existing per-measure "!" diagnostics — paste
  never re-times, splits or ties content. (MuseScore splits-and-ties across
  barlines; that is real re-notation — it changes the note count — and it
  is the deferred "rhythmic fit". It can arrive later as a paste-special
  without touching this contract.) Note the distinction D8 sharpened:
  *flowing whole items* across a barline is pure placement arithmetic and
  IS this contract's behavior; *splitting one item* is renotation and is
  not. Only a single duration that straddles the destination barline
  triggers the overfull flag now.
- **The fingerboard refusal is deleted.** An annotated note whose string/
  fret cannot sound on the destination instrument already renders as a red
  `scope: 'tab'` badge with a derived-fret fallback — the diagnostics layer
  was built for exactly this. Paste has no business pre-empting it.

## Decisions locked in this proposal

Each of these was a fork; the choice and its reason are the contract.

- **D1 — measures/section paste overwrites; it never inserts.** Today's
  behavior at a measure point is insert-before. One paste verb, always
  overwrite; "insert bars here" becomes an explicit command when wanted.
- **D2 — no split-and-tie renotation** (see land-and-flag). Overfull bars
  flag; a follow-up paste-special may renotate.
- **D3 — part bootstrapping on measures/section paste** (rule 4). Positional
  mapping, descriptor-driven creation, empty columns for uncovered parts.
- **D4 — voice-bars paste creates the missing sequence.** The old contract
  deferred sequence-creation policy to
  [core-entry-surface.md](../proposed/core-entry-surface.md); an always-lands paste
  cannot defer it. This is deliberately the **narrow** policy — create the
  sequence with exactly the pasted content on write — and it neither
  answers nor prejudges entry-surface's harder question (what *typing* into
  a missing voice means, mid-bar, with padding); that item still owns
  entry's answer and may adopt this write-path precedent or argue past it.
- **D5 — a score clip replaces the document,** guarded by undo, not by an
  emptiness precondition.
- **D6 — a note pasted on a rest takes the rest's duration** (the clip has
  none to bring).
- **D7 — a note pasted on a chord replaces the nearest-line member** —
  consistent with the floor-axis rule that a note selection is one notehead.
- **D8 — event/container runs FLOW (2026-08-20 amendment).** The first cut
  treated the clip's bar structure as authoritative — four quarters pasted
  at beat 3 made a six-quarter bar and a flag. Review judged that a score
  author's expectation is MuseScore's: the music continues across the
  barline, overwriting only what it covers. Flow is exactly that, and it is
  arithmetic, not renotation: each item's distance from the run's start is
  measured against the SOURCE meters (recorded per covered bar as
  `effectiveTime` in the clip's measure context — declared-or-inherited, so
  linearization never guesses), and the item lands in whichever destination
  bar its translated onset falls in, destination meters deciding where the
  barlines are. Whole items only: a duration that straddles stays whole and
  flags (D2). Gaps in sparse clips become the silent time they always were
  and make no statement about the material they skip. Cross-meter pastes
  stop manufacturing overfull bars — a 4/4 bar of quarters into 3/4 is a
  bar and a third. Spanning beams re-home to the bar their first event
  flowed into.

## Feedback: from refusals to accommodation records

The plan result's refusal codes largely become **accommodation records** —
the same typed, testable discipline, pointed at what the paste *did*:
bars appended, parts created, units consumed to rests, annotations flagged.
The [stage-6 notice strip](../complete/core-selection-clipboard.md) pivots
with it:

> `pasted event-run at bars 3–4 · 1 bar appended · tuplet tail → rests ·
> 2 frets flagged`

That report is what an authoritative paste owes the author before they
decide whether to Ctrl+Z. The two surviving refusals keep their sentences.

## Evidence when it lands

- The acceptance matrix inverts: every clip kind × every destination rung ×
  point/range/closure **lands**, and the only refusal tests left are empty
  store and undecodable clip.
- Accommodation records pinned per case: appended-bar counts, created
  part/sequence identities, consumed-unit counts, flag counts — and the
  notice sentences over them.
- Rest-fill spelling proven identical to Cut's clear-to-rests (one shared
  helper, one shared test).
- Cross-meter fixtures land byte-true and the overfull diagnostics fire;
  fingerboard fixtures land and the `scope: 'tab'` badges fire.
- Published MNX + `_x.mnxLab` validation after every accepted paste — the
  document is always schema-valid even when a bar is flagged overfull.
- Undo/redo restores document and selection across every accommodating
  paste; trace replay never consults the store (unchanged from stage 4).
- `npm run update:primitives` stays byte-clean.

## Deferred explicitly

- Tiling / repeat-to-fill into a destination extent.
- The paste-special family (pitch only, rhythm only, renotate-and-tie,
  merge-into-chord) — now the natural home for every "smarter" paste.
- Insert-mode paste (D1's other fork) as an explicit command.
- OS clipboard transport (unchanged from the parent item).
